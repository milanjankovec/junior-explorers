About Junior Explorers

How do you get the robot home? Who left those tracks in the woods? And how do you share a pizza so everyone gets an equal slice? A child can pick the question that catches their eye and see if they can figure it out. Those little quests for answers are how Junior Explorers began.

Why we made it

Junior Explorers started with a simple idea. Parents and teachers should have games they can open in seconds, where children have time to figure things out for themselves. Sometimes a child tackles a challenge on their own. Sometimes someone pulls up a chair and they try out ideas together.

The collection is mainly for children aged six to nine. Some will get hooked on a treasure hunt, while others will want to make a comic of their own. Every game has three difficulty levels, so children can come back to a favourite challenge once the first version feels easy.

What we want to give children

We want children to have time to think, try things out and change their minds as they play. If the robot ends up facing a wall, they can go back to the arrows and see which one sent it the wrong way. If one side of the scales tips down, they can take away a weight and see what happens. And when they work something out, they might want to show you how they did it.

In the browser and on paper

The games are free, with no ads or sign-up. You can play in your browser or print a worksheet for any game. You can also download the collection and use it offline.

The interface is available in 23 languages, so families and teachers can choose the language they usually use with their children. Vocabulary-based games come with Slovak, Czech and English word sets.
